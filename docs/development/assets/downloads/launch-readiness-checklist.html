Development launch-readiness checklist

Questions to answer before you bring a residential project to market.

Harrison Development · Development Marketing & Sales

For developers, builders and landowners. Tick what is true today. The gaps are your next steps, and a good list to bring to a first conversation.

1Project facts

  • Ownership and your authority to sell or market are documented.
  • Entitlement and approval status is written down, with dates and source documents.
  • Unit mix, sizes and phasing are defined.
  • Construction timeline and delivery dates are agreed with the builder.
  • Pricing or rental terms are decided, or the plan to decide them is set.

2Market and buyer

  • Comparable sales and competing projects have been reviewed.
  • Intended buyers or renters are described by what they need, in plain language.
  • Your price positioning has a written rationale.
  • Demand drivers, supply and seasonality are noted.

3Brand and materials

  • The project name has been searched for conflicts.
  • Site plan, floor plans and finishes are approved for marketing.
  • Renderings, and any AI-generated images, are labeled and match approved plans.
  • A website or landing page with an inquiry form is ready.
  • Rights to photos, video and music are confirmed.

4Sales readiness

  • A sales team or brokerage is chosen, with a written agreement.
  • Marketing fees and brokerage compensation are documented separately.
  • Required disclosures and fair-housing language are reviewed by your attorney and broker.
  • Reservation, contract, deposit and escrow steps are decided.
  • Showing plan, model or site-tour safety is set.

5Inquiry and follow-up

  • Every inquiry lands in one place, with a response-time goal.
  • Qualification questions are agreed.
  • Buyer feedback reaches the development team on a regular schedule.
  • You know what personal information you collect, why, and where it is stored.

6Compliance reminders

  • You advertise only what is authorized, accurate and approved.
  • Marketing avoids language that excludes people.
  • Claims about returns, appreciation or sell-out pace are avoided unless substantiated and approved.
  • Marketing copy is reviewed by someone other than its author.
