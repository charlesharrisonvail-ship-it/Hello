How to make a listing video without hiring a drone

AiRE Estate guide · Updated October 9, 2026

You have three options: hire a drone operator, buy a premium listing package, or generate an aerial-style clip with AI from a photo you own. Each one is right for a different listing.

What each option costs

OptionTypical costWhat you get
Licensed drone operator$250 to $550 per shoot, weather permittingReal aerial footage of the actual property and lot
Premium listing package on a major platform$400 to $950 per listingOften bundles a video with photos, a 3D tour, a floor plan and better placement on that platform
AI aerial-style video from one photoA few dollars of video credits per listing, plus your AI subscriptionsA short, cinematic clip generated from your photo. It is not real footage.

Ranges are publicly reported prices as of September 2026. They vary by market, home price and provider, so get a quote before you budget.

What AI aerial-style video is, and what it isn't

An AI video service takes one exterior photo and generates a moving shot that looks like a drone flight. It is fast and cheap.

It is also invented. These models sometimes add details that aren't there: an extra window, a different roofline, a view past the trees. That is fine for a lifestyle post. It is not fine in a property advertisement, where a buyer may rely on what they see.

Three rules if you use AI video on a listing
1. Watch every frame and cut anything that isn't true to the property.
2. Label it as AI-generated wherever you post it.
3. Use photos you have the rights to. Listing photos usually belong to the photographer or the listing brokerage.

When to hire the drone pilot anyway

Hire a licensed operator when the aerial view carries facts a buyer will rely on: acreage, lot lines, a view that drives the price, or the property's relationship to its neighbors. AI can't show those truthfully because it never saw them.
