Using AI to write listing descriptions? The fair housing rules still apply.

AiRE Estate Academy guide · Updated October 28, 2026

AI writes the draft. You publish the ad. The Fair Housing Act cares about the ad, not about who typed it.

The rule, in one sentence

The federal Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to make, print or publish any notice, statement or advertisement for the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin (42 U.S.C. § 3604(c)). That is seven federal protected classes. Many states and cities add more, so check yours.

Where AI drafts go wrong

AI learned to write from millions of old listings, and some old listings describe the buyer they wanted instead of the house. A draft can pick up that habit. The fix is one question: does this sentence describe the property, or the people who should buy it?

Describes the buyer (rewrite it)Describes the property (keep it)
Perfect for a young familyFour bedrooms, fenced backyard
Ideal for empty nestersSingle-level living, primary suite on the main floor
Great bachelor padOne-bedroom loft, 740 sq ft
Not suited to wheelchair usersThree steps up to the front entry

What changed at HUD, and what didn't

In 2024 HUD issued guidance on how the Fair Housing Act applies to ads delivered by algorithms and AI. HUD withdrew that guidance effective September 17, 2025, and published the withdrawal in the Federal Register on April 6, 2026. The statute did not change. Section 3604(c) still applies to your ad whether a person or software wrote it.

Boosting the listing online

If you pay to promote a listing on social media, tell the platform it is a housing ad when it asks. Platforms limit the audience choices for housing ads, and choosing who sees an ad is part of the ad.
