# Module 7 — Make It Part of Your Week

**Course:** Build Your AI Assistant · **Status:** draft manuscript · **Video:** not yet recorded

## 1. Learning objective and required inputs

**By the end of this module you can** run your assistant on a weekly routine, keep its
sources and instructions current, record changes, recognize when a human or qualified
professional must decide, and adapt the pattern to a second job.

**You need:**
- Your tested assistant and test log.
- `resources/weekly-maintenance-checklist.md`.
- Your calendar.

## 2. Narrated lesson script

*Narrator: AiRE. Target read time about 6 minutes.*

An assistant that works in a lesson and an assistant that works in your week are
different things. The second one has to survive busy Fridays, price changes, a new
brokerage disclosure, and you forgetting how you set it up three months from now.

So this last module is about routine. Three parts: a weekly run, a monthly check, and a
written record.

The weekly run. Put it on your calendar. Friday, two o'clock, seller updates. Before you
run it, gather the inputs: this week's showing export for each listing, any price change
paperwork, and any seller messages. Then run it, one listing at a time. And then the step
that never gets skipped: read every draft in full. Check the checklist. Answer every
missing, stale and agent-decision item yourself. Then you send. The assistant saved you
the typing and the assembly. It didn't save you the judgment.

Keep a short list of hand edits. If you fix the same thing by hand three Fridays in a
row, that's not you being picky. That's your instructions telling you they need an
update.

The monthly check. First Friday of the month. Reread your Voice Profile: does it still
sound like you? Rerun the ten-case test log and record the date. Remove reference files
for listings that closed or were withdrawn, because last spring's showing log has no
business being in the pack. And if you changed the instructions, bump the version number
and write one line in the change history: what changed, why, and whether you reran the
tests.

That change history sounds like paperwork. It's the thing that saves you in March, when
a draft does something odd and you need to know whether it's the tool, the sources, or
the line you added in January.

Now, the boundaries, one more time, because routine is when they slip. Your assistant
drafts. These things are never its decision: pricing and price changes; anything about
what a contract or a law means; whether to disclose something; how to handle a
complaint; and anything a seller asks you to say that isn't documented. When those come
up, the checklist says agent decision, and you decide, or you bring in your broker or an
attorney. A routine makes the easy parts automatic so you have time for the hard parts.

Finally, reuse. You built one assistant, but you also learned a pattern: one job, a
voice, a source pack, portable instructions, a test set, and a routine. That pattern
works for the next job too.

Take a buyer comparison. Same steps. Job card: turn the facts of two or three homes the
buyer toured into a side-by-side comparison, plus a checklist. Inputs: listing facts for
each home, the buyer's stated must-haves. Rules: only supplied facts; no school claims; no
statements about neighborhoods' people; no "you should buy this one." Test cases: a
missing HOA fee, a feature the buyer assumes but the listing doesn't state, a stale
listing.

Or listing copy. Job card: turn the listing worksheet into an MLS description and a
social caption. Rules: features from the worksheet only; fair-housing language check;
no "perfect for families." Tests: a feature the seller wishes were true, a missing room
dimension.

I want to be clear: those two aren't built in this course. They're outlines. If you
build one, run it through Modules 1 to 6 just like the seller update, including the test
set. Don't skip straight to production because the first one worked.

Your exercise: put the weekly run and the monthly check on your calendar, copy the
maintenance checklist, and write your Assistant Operating Guide: a one-page summary of
what your assistant does, where its files live, how to run it, who decides what, and when
it was last tested. If you were out sick, could a colleague run Friday's updates from
that page? That's the test.

Then head to the capstone.

## 4. Written walkthrough

1. Add two recurring calendar events: weekly ("Seller updates — run, review, send") and
   monthly, first Friday ("Assistant maintenance — test log, sources, voice").
2. Copy `weekly-maintenance-checklist.md` into your `seller-updates` folder.
3. Add a version line at the top of your master instructions (e.g., "Version 1.1 —
   2026-10-30") and a change-history table at the bottom (template in the checklist).
4. Write your Operating Guide using the template below. Save it in the `seller-updates`
   folder.
5. Optional: draft a Job Card for a buyer comparison or listing copy assistant. Mark it
   "outline — not yet built or tested."

## 5. Copy-ready template — Assistant Operating Guide

```
ASSISTANT OPERATING GUIDE — [Assistant name]   Version [x.y]   Last tested [date]

What it does (one sentence):
Where it lives: [Project name] / [folder path + /seller-update]
Inputs each run: [list + where to get them]
How to run: [3–5 steps]
Output: draft + review checklist. It never sends.
Who decides:
  - Pricing, strategy: [agent]
  - Contract or legal meaning: [broker / attorney]
  - Disclosures and complaints: [broker]
  - Seller requests to state undocumented facts: [agent; default is decline]
Weekly routine: [day/time; checklist link]
Monthly routine: [first Friday; test log; voice; remove closed listings]
Change history: [link or table]
If something looks wrong: stop, don't send, check sources, rerun test cases.
```

## 6. Practice exercise (fictional inputs)

1. Simulate a week: run all three Willow Fork properties, review each draft, and fill in
   the weekly checklist, including a hand-edit list.
2. Simulate a month: rerun the ten-case test, record the date, and add one change to the
   change history (for example, a sharper missing-fact line).
3. Write your Operating Guide.
4. Ask a colleague, friend or family member to read the guide and tell you the first step
   of a Friday run. If they can't, revise it.

## 7. Worked example — and why it meets the criteria

> **ASSISTANT OPERATING GUIDE — Friday Seller Update — v1.1 — last tested 2026-10-30**
> Turns approved listing facts and weekly showing exports into a draft seller update and
> review checklist. Lives in Claude Project "Friday Seller Updates" and folder
> `~/seller-updates` (`/seller-update <folder>`). Inputs: showing export (showing service,
> Fridays), listing facts (listing file; recheck after price changes), seller messages.
> Run: export showings → save to listing folder → run skill per listing → review each
> draft and checklist → answer flags → send from my email. Never sends on its own.
> Decides: pricing and strategy — me with seller; contract/legal meaning — broker or
> attorney; disclosures and complaints — broker; undocumented seller claims — decline.
> Weekly: Fri 2 p.m. Monthly: first Friday, test log + voice + cleanup.
> Change history: v1.1 (Oct. 30) — named the HOA field in the missing-fact rule after two
> hand edits; all ten cases rerun, pass.

**Why it meets the criteria:** a colleague could run it from this page; decisions are
assigned to people; it states it never sends; and the change history records what
changed, why and that tests were rerun.

## 8. Troubleshooting and completion checklist

**I skipped a week and now it feels like work.** Shorten it: one listing at a time,
same day each week. The routine is the point.

**My pack keeps growing.** Monthly cleanup: remove closed listings and anything not on
your Job Card.

**A colleague wants to use my assistant.** Share the Operating Guide and instructions.
They need their own Voice Profile and should rerun the test set with their own setup.

**Completion checklist**
- [ ] Weekly and monthly events on the calendar.
- [ ] Maintenance checklist copied and used once.
- [ ] Version number and change history in the instructions.
- [ ] Operating Guide written and read by someone else.
- [ ] Decisions assigned to the right humans.

## 9. Module check

### Q1. You've fixed the same issue by hand three Fridays in a row. What does that tell you?
- A) You're too picky.
- B) The instructions need updating; then rerun the tests.
- C) The assistant is broken and should be deleted.
- D) Nothing.
**Answer:** B — Repeated hand edits are a signal to fix the instructions.

### Q2. Which is never the assistant's decision?
- A) Formatting showing counts as digits.
- B) Whether to recommend a price reduction.
- C) Paraphrasing feedback without names.
- D) Keeping the draft under 220 words.
**Answer:** B — Pricing is the agent's and seller's decision.

### Q3. Why keep a change history with dates and versions?
- A) It's required by Claude.
- B) So when something odd happens later, you can tell whether the tool, the sources or an instruction change caused it.
- C) To make the instructions longer.
- D) Sellers ask for it.
**Answer:** B — Change history makes problems traceable.

### Q4. What should the monthly check include?
- A) Only reading the Voice Profile.
- B) Rerunning the test log, reviewing the voice, removing closed listings, and recording changes.
- C) Deleting the project.
- D) Asking the AI whether it's still working.
**Answer:** B — Testing and cleanup keep it reliable.

### Q5. You want a buyer-comparison assistant. What's the right approach?
- A) Copy the seller-update instructions and use them unchanged.
- B) Run the same pattern: Job Card, voice, sources, instructions, test set, routine.
- C) Skip testing because the first assistant worked.
- D) Let it recommend which home to buy.
**Answer:** B — The pattern transfers; the tests don't. Each new assistant gets its own.

### Q6. A seller asks whether a crack in the foundation must be disclosed. What does the checklist say?
- A) The assistant answers based on general knowledge.
- B) AGENT DECISION; the agent consults the broker or an attorney as needed.
- C) Nothing; it's not in the inputs.
- D) "No disclosure needed."
**Answer:** B — Disclosure questions carry legal weight. A human decides.

### Q7. What's the test of a good Operating Guide?
- A) It's at least five pages.
- B) A colleague could run Friday's updates from it.
- C) It uses technical terms.
- D) It's stored only in your head.
**Answer:** B — If someone else can run it, you've written it down well enough.
