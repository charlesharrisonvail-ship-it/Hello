# Capstone Rubric — Lead to Keys: The Final Project

**This is a self-assessment.** No instructor reviews your project. Score yourself honestly
against the criteria below; the rubric exists so you can see exactly what "done right" means
before you start. Your Lead to Keys certificate of completion still comes from the module
checks (seven questions per module, five right to pass). It is a private certificate of
completion, not a continuing-education credit or an association designation.

## What you build

One property: your own listing, a friend's home with their permission, or your own house.

1. A clean fact sheet, every gap flagged.
2. Three descriptions (MLS, social, showcase) that pass your fair-housing read.
3. Part One students: a drone clip and a listing showcase, both labeled as AI-made (optional).
4. A comparative market analysis with a source under every number.
5. A ten-slide listing presentation.
6. An open house week and a follow-up message.
7. A blank transaction checklist, ready for the day it goes under contract.

## Score each criterion 0, 1 or 2

| Criterion | 0 — Not yet | 1 — Partly | 2 — Done right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fact sheet** | Facts are missing or unsourced, and gaps are filled with guesses | Facts are sourced, but some gaps aren't flagged | Every fact has a source; every gap is flagged, not guessed |
| **Descriptions** | A description adds a feature not on the fact sheet, or fails the fair-housing read | All three are accurate, but one reads as generic or off-voice | Three accurate, distinct descriptions in your voice that pass the fair-housing read |
| **Market analysis** | A number has no source, or it's presented as an appraisal | Every number is sourced, but adjustments aren't explained | A source under every number, adjustments explained, labeled as your analysis, not an appraisal |
| **Listing presentation** | Ten slides, but claims can't be traced to the fact sheet or the analysis | Most claims trace back; one slide overstates | Every claim on all ten slides traces to the fact sheet or the analysis |
| **Follow-up** | A message would go to people without consent, or breaks texting or email rules | Rules are followed, but there's no dated next step | Open-house week planned; the follow-up respects consent and carries a dated next step |
| **Transaction checklist** | Missing key deadlines or the wire-fraud warning | Deadlines listed, but owners or reminders are missing | Every deadline with an owner and a reminder, plus the wire-fraud warning |

## Passing

- **Passing score: 10 out of 12**, with **no critical failure**.
- **Critical failures** (any one means not yet, whatever the score):
  - A fact, price or number appears that isn't in your sources (fabricated fact).
  - Anything in the package fails your fair-housing read.
  - The plan would contact anyone without the consent your texting and email rules require.

## If you're not there yet

| Weak spot | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Fact sheet | Rebuild it from your sources and flag every blank. Revisit Module 1, The Listing, On Paper. |
| Descriptions | Rewrite against the fact sheet only, then reread for fair housing. Revisit Module 1, The Listing, On Paper. |
| Market analysis | Put a source under every number; label it your analysis. Revisit Module 3, The Numbers, Properly. |
| Listing presentation | Trace each slide's claims to the fact sheet or the analysis. Revisit Module 4, The Seller Side. |
| Follow-up | Check consent for every contact and add a dated next step. Revisit Module 6, Staying in Front of People. |
| Transaction checklist | Add owners, reminders and the wire-fraud line. Revisit Module 7, Under Contract. |
| Critical failure | Fix the cause, then rescore the whole package. |
