# Short Script Templates

Three patterns, each about 15–30 seconds. Word count is only a starting guess: read the
script aloud at your spokesperson's pace and time it. Every example below is fictional
(agent "Sam Reyes," Willow Fork Realty, the town of Willow Fork). Replace everything in
brackets.

Every script has four parts: **hook** (first 2–3 seconds), **one useful point**,
**evidence where a claim needs it**, and **one call to action**.

---

## Pattern 1 — Introduction (with AI disclosure)

**Template**

> **Hook:** "[Line that names who it's for and what they get.]"
> **Point:** "I'm [NAME] with [BROKERAGE]. I help [WHO] with [WHAT] in [AREA]."
> **Disclosure:** "Quick note: this is an AI version of me, made from my own recording,
> so I can post short tips more often. Every word is mine and I review every video."
> **CTA:** "[ONE action.]"

**Worked example (fictional, ~24 seconds read aloud)**

> If you're selling in Willow Fork in the next year, here's who I am.
> I'm Sam Reyes with Willow Fork Realty. I help sellers get their home ready, priced and
> in front of the right buyers.
> Quick note: this is an AI version of me, made from my own recording, so I can post short
> tips more often. I write and review every one.
> If you want a straight answer about your home, send me a message.

*Why it works:* the first line says who it's for; the disclosure is plain and early; one
CTA. *Fixed:* no claims about results, rankings or "number one agent."

---

## Pattern 2 — Educational tip

**Template**

> **Hook:** "[A common mistake or question in one sentence.]"
> **Point:** "[The one thing to do instead.]"
> **Evidence:** "[Only if you state a fact: say where it comes from, or keep it general.]"
> **CTA:** "[ONE action.]"
> **On-screen label:** "Made with AI"

**Worked example (fictional, ~22 seconds)**

> Before you list, find your HOA documents. Not next week — now.
> Buyers and their agents often ask for the HOA budget, the current fee and any special
> assessments early, and missing paperwork can slow everything down.
> Your HOA or management company can usually send current copies.
> Want the checklist I give my sellers? Comment "HOA" and I'll send it.

*Why it works:* one point, practical, no statistics that would need a source. Note the
careful words "often" and "can," not "always" or "will." *Verify* any rule-based claim
(for example, a state's disclosure timeline) against an official source before you add it.

## Pattern 3 — Listing invitation (fictional practice listing)

**Template**

> **Hook:** "[One true, specific feature — from the listing sheet.]"
> **Point:** "[Address/area], [beds/baths], [one or two more facts from the listing sheet]."
> **Evidence:** facts only from the approved listing sheet.
> **CTA:** "[Showing / open house details, or 'message me for a showing.']"
> **Required:** brokerage identification as your state and brokerage require.

**Worked example (fictional practice listing, ~20 seconds)**

> A backyard you'll actually use, in Willow Fork.
> 1418 Juniper Lane: four bedrooms, two and a half baths, about 2,240 finished square feet,
> and a two-car garage.
> Open house Sunday, one to three.
> Message me for details. Sam Reyes, Willow Fork Realty.

*Why it works:* every fact matches the practice listing sheet (Property A). No claims about
schools, "perfect for families," or neighborhood character. *Note:* if this were a real
listing, you would need the seller's permission to market it and must follow your MLS and
brokerage advertising rules.

---

## Never in any script

- Invented testimonials or quotes ("My clients always say…") unless real, permitted and verifiable.
- Firsthand experiences the speaker didn't have (a fictional spokesperson never says "when I sold my house").
- Guaranteed results ("we'll sell it in 30 days," "top dollar guaranteed").
- Statements about who should live somewhere, school quality claims, or neighborhood "type."
- Statistics without a source you can name.
