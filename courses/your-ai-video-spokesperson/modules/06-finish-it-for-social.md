# Module 6 — Finish It for Social

**Course:** Your AI Video Spokesperson · **Status:** draft manuscript · **Video:** not yet recorded

## 1. Learning objective and required inputs

**By the end of this module you can** finish each video for social: readable bold
captions in safe areas, a logo, purposeful B-roll, music balanced under speech, and a
separate vertical export for each video, with a clean version where practical.

**You need:**
- Your rendered talking videos (Module 5).
- A video editor you already have. Your provider's built-in editor may be enough; a free
  editor works too. No second paid tool is required.
- `resources/captions-and-audio-checklist.md` and `resources/shot-and-edit-plan.csv`.
- Licensed music (optional) and your logo file.
- Seller-permitted listing photos for B-roll (or the course's fictional placeholders).

## 2. Narrated lesson script

*Narrator: AiRE. Target read time about 6–7 minutes.*

You've got a talking video. Now let's make it work where people actually watch: on a
phone, scrolling, often with the sound off.

Captions first, because for many viewers, captions are the video. Bold, high contrast,
big enough to read at arm's length. No more than two lines at a time. And placed in the
safe area. Every platform puts its own buttons, profile picture and text over the bottom
and right side of a vertical video. If your captions sit there, they're covered. Keep
them in the middle band of the frame.

Captions have to match what's spoken. Automatic transcription is a great start and a bad
finish. It will spell your brokerage wrong, turn "Heron Point" into "Herring Point," and
write "$612" when you said six hundred twelve thousand. Correct every caption by hand
against the script and the source. Where your editor allows it, keep the captions as
editable text in your project, so a typo next week doesn't mean a new render.

Next, your brand. Logo in a corner that doesn't collide with captions or platform buttons.
Brokerage identification as your rules require, in the same place every time. And the
"Made with AI" label for the whole video.

B-roll is where movement comes from. A listing photo with a slow push-in. A simple card
with the facts: four bedrooms, two and a half baths. Cut to B-roll when it shows
something the words describe. Only use photos you have permission to use, which for
listings means the seller's authorization and your MLS rules, and match every fact card
to the listing sheet.

Music, if you use it, sits under speech. Speech first, always. A good starting point is
music well below the voice; then trust your ears. Here's the test that matters: play it
on your phone's speaker at a normal volume. If any word is hard to understand, the music
is too loud. Use only music licensed for commercial social use.

Now, a common shortcut I want you to skip: merging all three videos into one grid or one
long video. Each idea gets its own nine-by-sixteen export. Separate files let you post
them on different days, test which hook works, and fix one without touching the others.

And keep a clean version where you can: the video without captions and music. When
something changes next month, you'll be glad you have it.

Finally, captions must match the spoken facts. Not more, not less. A caption that adds a
detail the voice didn't say, or rounds a number differently, is a mismatch someone will
notice. Check every number on screen against the source one more time.

Your exercise: finish all three videos, using the shot and edit plan, and run the
captions and audio checklist on each. Name your files clearly. Then watch all three on
your phone, sound off, then sound on.

## 4. Written walkthrough

Editors differ, so these steps describe what to do rather than which button. Check your
editor's help for exact controls.

1. Open your rendered video in your editor. Set the project to 9:16 (e.g., 1080×1920).
2. Add captions (built-in auto-captions are fine as a start).
3. Correct every caption against the script: names, places, numbers, dates.
4. Style captions bold and high-contrast; max two lines; move them into the middle band.
5. Add your logo and "Made with AI" label away from captions and platform UI zones.
6. Add B-roll per your shot and edit plan; check each fact card against the source.
7. Add licensed music (optional) under the voice; phone-speaker test; adjust.
8. Export one 9:16 file per video. If practical, export a clean version (no captions, no music).
9. Name files: `2026-10-30_intro_9x16_captioned.mp4`, `..._clean.mp4`.
10. Run the captions and audio checklist for each file.

## 8. Troubleshooting and completion checklist

**Captions get covered by buttons.** Move them up into the middle band.

**I can't hear the voice on my phone.** Lower the music. Then lower it again.

**Completion checklist**
- [ ] Captions corrected and in safe areas.
- [ ] Logo, brokerage ID and "Made with AI" label placed.
- [ ] B-roll and fact cards checked against sources.
- [ ] Phone speaker test passed.
- [ ] Three separate 9:16 exports; clean version where practical.

## 9. Module check

### Q1. Where should captions sit on a vertical social video?
- A) At the very bottom edge.
- B) In the middle band, clear of the platform's buttons and text.
- C) Across the speaker's face.
- D) Captions aren't needed.
**Answer:** B — Bottom and right edges are often covered by platform UI.

### Q2. Auto-captions spelled the street "Herring Point." What should you do?
- A) Leave it; viewers will understand.
- B) Correct every caption by hand against the script and source.
- C) Remove all captions.
- D) Re-render the avatar.
**Answer:** B — Automated transcription is a start, not a finish.

### Q3. How loud should music be?
- A) As loud as the voice.
- B) Clearly under speech; every word understandable on a phone speaker.
- C) Louder for energy.
- D) Music is required.
**Answer:** B — Speech first.

### Q4. Why export three separate 9:16 videos instead of one merged grid?
- A) Grids aren't allowed.
- B) Separate files let you post, test and fix each one independently.
- C) It's cheaper to render.
- D) Platforms delete grids.
**Answer:** B — One idea, one video, one file.

### Q5. A caption shows a detail the voice didn't say. What's the issue?
- A) None; more information is better.
- B) Captions must match the spoken facts; a mismatch invites errors and confusion.
- C) It should be bigger.
- D) It should be in a different color.
**Answer:** B — Not more, not less.

### Q6. Which B-roll is appropriate for a real listing video?
- A) Any home photo from the internet.
- B) Listing photos you're authorized to use, with fact cards matched to the listing sheet.
- C) Photos of a different, nicer house.
- D) A competitor's listing photos.
**Answer:** B — Permission and accuracy.

### Q7. Why keep a clean version (no captions or music)?
- A) It's required by law.
- B) To make future edits without re-rendering the talking video.
- C) To post twice.
- D) Clean versions get more views.
**Answer:** B — It saves time and credits later.
