# Module 5 — Produce the Talking Video

**Course:** Your AI Video Spokesperson · **Status:** draft manuscript · **Video:** not yet recorded

## 1. Learning objective and required inputs

**By the end of this module you can** create your avatar (own-likeness or fictional),
generate one short talking video from an approved script with an authorized voice,
review it for identity, lip sync, pronunciation and unintended changes, and diagnose a
failed output without endless re-rendering.

**You need:**
- A HeyGen account (free or paid; check current plans and limits at heygen.com).
- Own likeness: your 2–5 minute footage (Module 2) and a quiet place to record a consent
  video. Fictional route: your licensed character and voice.
- Your Style Sheet, Pronunciation Sheet and one approved script (Module 4).
- `resources/pre-render-checklist.md`.

**Primary workflow:** HeyGen. Optional B-roll from another tool you already use (for
example, Higgsfield, where AiRE Estate Academy has a labeled affiliate link) is never required to
complete the course.

## 2. Narrated lesson script

*Narrator: Eva Vail, AiRE Estate Academy's AI presenter, using owner-approved Eva assets and voice. Target read time about 7–8 minutes.*

Everything up to now was preparation: the route, the look, the voice and the script.
This is where it becomes a video. We'll make one, start to finish, and then we'll talk
about what to do when it comes out wrong, because sometimes it will.

Step one is the avatar. On the own-likeness route in HeyGen, as of the help center we
checked for this lesson, you go to the Avatars area, choose New Avatar, and then Clone a
Real Person. You can record live or import footage. Use the continuous take you filmed
in Module 2. Then comes the consent video: you read HeyGen's exact consent text, on
camera, under thirty seconds, and it's submitted with the footage. HeyGen's help center
says processing takes roughly ten to twenty minutes for non-4K footage. While you wait,
re-read your script.

A note worth knowing before you click: HeyGen's help center says each avatar slot can be
recreated once per billing cycle, and deleting an avatar removes all of its looks. So
don't upload footage you're unsure about just to see what happens. Fix the footage first.

On the fictional route, you'll select your licensed character instead, and the voice
you licensed.

Step two: create the video. Pick your avatar and the look that matches your Style Sheet:
the master look, or one of your two planned settings. Paste the approved script, the
version with the respellings from your Pronunciation Sheet. Choose your authorized voice.
Set the aspect ratio to vertical, nine by sixteen, for Reels, Stories and TikTok.

Step three, before you render: the pre-render checklist. It takes two minutes, and every
item is something that costs credits or minutes to discover afterward. Script timed,
facts sourced, disclosure included, voice authorized, pronunciation tested, correct look,
correct aspect ratio, and you know what this render costs on your plan.

Step four: render, then review. Watch it four times, each time for one thing.

First pass: identity. Does it look like the master look? Any warping around the eyes,
mouth, hands, glasses or jewelry?

Second pass: lip sync. Watch the mouth on words with p, b and m. Does the mouth close?

Third pass: pronunciation. Your name, brokerage, places, numbers.

Fourth pass: unintended changes. Did anything appear that you didn't ask for? A gesture
pointing at nothing, an expression that doesn't fit the line, a background shift?

Now, about movement. You'll see AI videos where the avatar strolls around a kitchen,
turns, gestures at a window. It looks impressive once, and strange the third time. In
this course we favor a natural, stationary presentation. When you want movement, cut to
B-roll: a listing photo with a slow push-in, a simple graphic. Purposeful cuts beat
artificial walking.

And when it fails. Diagnose before you re-render. If lip sync drifts on one long
sentence, shorten the sentence. If one word is wrong, fix the respelling and preview that
line. If the face looks off in every video, the problem is the footage, not the script,
and no number of re-renders will fix it. Set yourself a rule: two attempts at the same
problem, then stop and diagnose. Endless re-rendering is how a free tool turns into an
expensive one.

Your exercise: make your first video from your approved introduction script. Do the
four-pass review and write down what you found. If it needs a fix, write the diagnosis
first, then make one change and re-render once.

## 4. Written walkthrough

**Verified October 23, 2026** against HeyGen's help center: "Create your first Digital
Twin (Video Avatar) with Avatar IV!", "Recording your Consent Video," "Digital Twin FAQ."
Steps 6–8 follow HeyGen's general description (pick an avatar, paste your script, choose a
voice); exact editor labels for aspect ratio and export **were not verified** and must be
confirmed in your account.

**Create the avatar (own likeness):**
1. Log in to HeyGen. Go to **Avatars** and select **New Avatar**, then **Clone a Real Person**.
2. Choose to record live footage or import your existing continuous take.
3. Record the consent video: read the provider's consent text exactly, under 30 seconds,
   clear light and sound. It must be you (the same person as the footage).
4. Submit. Processing takes about 10–20 minutes for non-4K footage (per HeyGen).
5. Remember: each avatar slot can be recreated once per billing cycle; deleting removes all looks.

**Create the video:**
6. Start a new video; choose your avatar and the look matching your Style Sheet.
7. Paste your approved script (with respellings). Choose your authorized voice.
8. Set the aspect ratio to 9:16 (vertical) if available for your plan.
9. Run the pre-render checklist. Preview if available.
10. Render. Download the file.

**Review (four passes):** identity; lip sync (p, b, m); pronunciation; unintended changes.
Record findings in your notes.

```
One change I'll make:
Re-render?  Yes (attempt 2 of 2) / No — stop and fix footage or settings
```

## 7. Worked example — and why it meets the criteria

> VIDEO: Sam intro · RENDER 1
> Identity OK. Lip sync: drifts on "so I can post short tips more often and I write and
> review every one" (long sentence). Pronunciation: "Willow Fork Realty" OK. Unintended:
> raised hand pointing at nothing at 0:14.
> Diagnosis: script (long sentence) + gesture. One change: split the sentence into two.
> RENDER 2: lip sync OK; gesture gone after the split. Approved for finishing.

**Why it meets the criteria:** all four passes were checked; the cause was identified
before re-rendering; one change fixed both issues; it stopped at two renders.

## 8. Troubleshooting and completion checklist

See `resources/troubleshooting.md`. The short version: fix footage problems at the
footage, script problems in the script, and stop after two renders of the same issue.

**Completion checklist**
- [ ] Avatar created with your own consent (or licensed fictional avatar).
- [ ] One video rendered from an approved script.
- [ ] Four-pass review logged.
- [ ] No more than two renders for the same issue.
