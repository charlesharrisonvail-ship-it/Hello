## 2. Narrated lesson script

*Narrator: Eva Vail, AiRE Estate's AI presenter, using owner-approved Eva assets and voice. Target read time about 6 minutes.*

You've made three videos. The fourth one should be faster, and the tenth should feel
routine. That only happens if you save what you've built and turn it into a process.

Start with templates. In your video tool, save a project for each pattern: introduction,
tip, listing invitation. Same avatar look, same caption style, same logo and AI label
positions, same end card. Next time, you duplicate the template and change the script.
In your editor, do the same with your caption style and lower thirds. And keep your
sheets together: Spokesperson Brief, Style Sheet, Pronunciation Sheet, Script Pack,
checklists. That folder is your production kit.

Variations, carefully. You planned two settings in Module 2. Use them. When you want
something new, a seasonal background or a different jacket for winter, add it as a
planned setting and change one thing. Test it against the master on your phone before
you use it for real.

Batching. Instead of making one video every few days, set aside one session to write
four scripts, one session to render them, and one to finish them. Batching saves setup
time, and it makes you check facts in one focused pass instead of in a hurry.

That's where the content calendar comes in. Ten rows, one per video. For each: the topic,
the pattern, the audience, the key fact or point, the source to check, and the date you
checked it. Then three status columns: script, render, approval. And the publish date,
platform and link once it's live. The approval column matters. Nothing publishes until
it says approved, and approved means you watched the finished file, with captions, on
your phone.

A template doesn't validate its claims. A saved listing-invitation template with last
month's open-house time is how wrong information goes out confidently. Every new video
gets the same checks: facts against sources, pronunciation, the four-pass review,
captions against speech.

Finally, measuring. Your platforms show views, how long people watched, comments,
messages and clicks. Look at them every couple of weeks. Which hooks kept people past
three seconds? Which topics brought messages? Use that to choose the next ten topics.

But I want to be clear about what measurement can and can't tell you. These numbers
describe what happened. They don't promise what will happen next. No template, hook or
posting time guarantees views, leads or listings, and anyone who tells you otherwise is
selling something. Measure, learn, adjust.

Your exercise: save your three templates, fill in a ten-topic calendar using your own
market, and write your repeat-production checklist: the steps you'll follow for every
new video, in order. That's your reusable production kit.

## 4. Written walkthrough

1. In your video tool, duplicate each finished video as a template, or use its template
   or duplicate feature if available (verify in your account). Name them
   `TEMPLATE_intro`, `TEMPLATE_tip`, `TEMPLATE_listing`.
2. In your editor, save caption style, lower-third and end-card presets.
3. Create a `production-kit` folder with your Brief, Rights Checklist, Style Sheet,
   Pronunciation Sheet, Script Pack, checklists and shot and edit plan.
4. Copy `video-content-calendar.csv`; replace the example topics with ten of your own.
5. For each topic, fill in the source to check and leave status as "Not started."
6. Write your repeat-production checklist (template below).
7. Every two weeks, record basic results per video (views, average watch time, comments,
   messages) in your own notes. Choose the next topics from what you learn.

## 5. Copy-ready template — repeat-production checklist

```
FOR EVERY NEW VIDEO
1. Calendar row: topic, pattern, audience, source to check.
2. Check the source; record the date.
3. Write the script from the pattern template; time it aloud (15–30 s).
4. Pronunciation check for new names/places/numbers (short preview).
5. Duplicate the video template; paste script; confirm look and voice.
6. Pre-render checklist. Render.
7. Four-pass review (identity, lip sync, pronunciation, unintended).
8. Finish: captions corrected, logo, AI label, B-roll checked, music under speech.
9. Captions and audio checklist; phone sound-off and sound-on test.
10. Mark Approved only after steps 1–9. Then publish and record the link.
```

**Why it meets the criteria:** the source is checked and dated; status columns show each
gate; approval followed the phone review; the time-sensitive fact was re-checked even
though the template had a previous time in it.

## 8. Troubleshooting and completion checklist

**Batching made me rush facts.** Separate the fact-check step from the writing step.

**A video "flopped."** One video's numbers aren't a verdict. Look across several, then
adjust hooks and topics.

**Completion checklist**
- [ ] Three templates saved.
- [ ] Production kit folder complete.
- [ ] Ten-topic calendar with sources.
- [ ] Repeat-production checklist written.

## 9. Module check

### Q1. What is a "production kit" in this course?
- A) A camera bundle.
- B) Your saved templates plus all your sheets and checklists, kept together.
- C) A paid add-on.
- D) A single video.
**Answer:** B — It makes the next video faster and consistent.

### Q2. You want a winter look. What's the right approach?
- A) Change wardrobe, hair and background together.
- B) Add a planned setting that changes one element; test against the master.
- C) Create a new avatar.
- D) Use a different person.
**Answer:** B — Controlled variation keeps identity.

### Q3. Why batch production?
- A) Platforms require it.
- B) It saves setup time and lets you check facts in one focused pass.
- C) It guarantees more views.
- D) Batching isn't recommended.
**Answer:** B — Efficiency and accuracy, no guarantees.

### Q4. When can a calendar row be marked "Approved"?
- A) As soon as the script is written.
- B) After the finished file passes all checks and you've reviewed it on your phone.
- C) When the render finishes.
- D) When a colleague likes the topic.
**Answer:** B — Approval follows review of the finished video.

### Q5. A saved listing template contains last week's open-house time. What does this tell you?
- A) Templates are always accurate.
- B) A template doesn't validate its claims; every new video needs fact checks.
- C) Don't use templates.
- D) Keep the old time.
**Answer:** B — Templates save time, not verification.

### Q6. What can platform analytics tell you?
- A) Exactly how the next video will perform.
- B) What happened with past videos, to inform your next topics and hooks.
- C) Guaranteed lead counts.
- D) Nothing useful.
**Answer:** B — Numbers describe; they don't promise.

### Q7. Which statement is consistent with this course?
- A) "This hook guarantees 10,000 views."
- B) "Posting at 7 p.m. guarantees leads."
