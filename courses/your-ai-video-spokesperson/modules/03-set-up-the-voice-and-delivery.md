# Module 3 — Set Up the Voice and Delivery

**Course:** Your AI Video Spokesperson · **Status:** draft manuscript · **Video:** not yet recorded

**By the end of this module you can** set up an authorized voice, test pronunciation of

## 2. Narrated lesson script

*Narrator: Eva Vail, AiRE Estate's AI presenter, using owner-approved Eva assets and voice. Target read time about 6 minutes.*

Viewers forgive a lot in a short video. A slightly stiff gesture, a plain background.
What they don't forgive is a voice that gets your own town wrong. Say "Willow Fork"
wrong once and every local viewer knows nobody checked.

So this module is about the voice: getting the right one, and making it say things
right.

First, the right one. On the own-likeness route, the voice should be yours. With HeyGen,
as of the help center we checked, each video avatar includes voice cloning from your
footage, and you can also create a separate voice from a clean audio recording. Either
way, it's your voice, recorded by you. One detail from HeyGen's guidance is easy to miss:
even if you plan to use a different voice, your filming footage should include you
speaking, because that helps lip sync.

On the fictional route, use a stock or licensed voice whose terms allow commercial use,
and keep a copy of those terms. Never a voice that imitates a real person. And consent
requirements and voice features change, so verify them with the provider before you rely
on them.

Now, making it say things right. You'll build a short pronunciation sheet. Seven rows:
your name, your brokerage, two towns or neighborhoods, the street names in this week's
scripts, numbers, and your call-to-action line.

Write a fifteen-second test script that uses all of them. Something like: "Hi, I'm Sam
Reyes with Willow Fork Realty. This is Willow Fork, and I'm on Heron Point Court. If
you're thinking about selling this spring, message me." Then preview just that. Not a
full video. A short preview costs little or nothing, and it'll catch most problems.

Here's what you'll hear in our example. "Willow Fork," fine. "Heron Point," said
"HAIR-on." Not how locals say it. The fix: respell the word for the voice in the script,
"Herron Point," and preview again. Your captions keep the correct spelling; only the
spoken script gets the respelling. If your provider offers a pronunciation or phonetic
setting, that's even better, but check how it works in your account; we didn't rely on
one for this lesson.

Numbers deserve their own test. "$612,000" can come out as "six one two thousand" or "six
hundred twelve thousand dollars." Write numbers in the script the way you want them
spoken, and keep the captions showing the correct figures.

Then pacing. Most AI voices default to a steady, slightly fast read. For short real
estate videos, slightly slower than you think usually lands better, with a pause after
the hook and another before the call to action. Some providers let you insert pauses or
adjust speed; others respond to punctuation, so a period or a line break creates a beat.
Try both and listen.

Listen three ways: laptop, headphones, and your phone's speaker at normal volume. The
phone speaker is where most of your audience will hear it. If a word is mushy there,
fix it.

Your exercise: fill in the pronunciation sheet, write your fifteen-second test script,
preview it, fix every problem, and record what you changed. Keep the sheet; you'll reuse
it for every video.

## 4. Written walkthrough

**Verified October 23, 2026** against HeyGen's help center ("Digital Twin FAQ," "Create
your first Digital Twin"). Voice features vary by plan and change over time; check in your
account.

1. Confirm the voice is authorized: your own (own-likeness route) or licensed for
   commercial use (fictional route). Save the license or note.
2. Own likeness with HeyGen: your video avatar includes voice cloning from your footage;
   you can also create a separate voice from a clean audio file (see HeyGen's voices
   help articles).
3. Fill in the Pronunciation Sheet's test words.
4. Write a 15-second test script using all of them.
5. Generate a short preview only. Listen on laptop, headphones and phone speaker.
6. For each mispronounced word, respell it in the script (not the caption) or use a
   pronunciation setting if your provider has one. Preview again.
7. Write numbers as you want them spoken.
8. Adjust pace and add pauses (punctuation, line breaks, or the provider's pause control).
9. Record what you changed in the sheet.

## 5. Copy-ready prompt (to prepare test scripts)

```
Write a 15-second test script (read aloud, about 35–45 words) for an AI voice. It must
include each of these exactly once, in natural sentences: [NAME], [BROKERAGE], [TOWN 1],
[TOWN 2], [STREET NAME], [A PRICE, written as words], and this call to action: [CTA].
No claims about results, schools or neighborhoods. Return only the script.
```

## 7. Worked example — and why it meets the criteria

**Test script:** "Hi, I'm Sam Reyes with Willow Fork Realty. I'm on Copper Bend Road in
Willow Fork, near Herron Point Court. One home here is listed at four hundred forty-nine
thousand, five hundred dollars. Message me for a showing."
**Caption for the same line:** "…listed at $449,500. Message me for a showing."
**Sheet entry:** Heron Point — preview said "HAIR-on" → script respelled "Herron"; caption
unchanged; re-previewed OK on phone speaker.

**Why it meets the criteria:** every critical word is tested in one short preview; the fix
is in the spoken script only; captions keep the correct spelling and figure.

## 8. Troubleshooting and completion checklist

**The voice sounds robotic on a phone.** Slow it slightly, add pauses, re-preview.

**My cloned voice has room echo.** Record a cleaner audio sample in a quieter space.

**Completion checklist**
- [ ] Voice authorization recorded.
- [ ] Pronunciation sheet complete.
- [ ] Test script previewed and fixed.
- [ ] Listened on phone speaker.

## 9. Module check

### Q1. Why test pronunciation with a short preview before rendering a full video?
- A) Previews are always free.
- B) It catches most problems cheaply, before spending on a full render.
- C) Providers require it.
- D) Full renders can't be changed.
**Answer:** B — Short previews are the cheap place to find problems.

### Q2. The voice says "HAIR-on Point" instead of the local "Heron Point." What's the fix?
- A) Change the caption to "Herron."
- B) Respell it in the spoken script (or use a pronunciation setting), keep the caption correct, and preview again.
- C) Rename the street.
- D) Ignore it.
**Answer:** B — Spoken script and caption serve different jobs.

### Q3. Which voice is authorized for a fictional spokesperson?
- A) A voice that imitates a famous actor.
- B) A stock or licensed voice whose terms allow commercial use.
- C) Your colleague's voice without asking.
- D) Eva Vail's voice.
**Answer:** B — Keep the license.

### Q4. Per HeyGen's guidance (Oct. 23, 2026), why should your filming footage include you speaking even if you'll use a different voice?
- A) It's needed for lip sync.
- B) It sets the background.
- C) It isn't necessary.
- D) It changes the price.
**Answer:** A — The help center flags speech in footage as crucial for lip sync.

### Q5. How should you write "$612,000" in the spoken script?
- A) Exactly as "$612K."
- B) The way you want it said, e.g., "six hundred twelve thousand dollars," while the caption shows "$612,000."
- C) Leave numbers out of videos.
- D) Round to "about six hundred thousand."
**Answer:** B — Spell numbers for speech; keep figures correct in captions.

### Q6. Where should you test playback most carefully?
- A) Studio monitors only.
- B) A phone speaker at normal volume, where most viewers listen.
- C) A car stereo.
- D) No need to listen.
**Answer:** B — Plus laptop and headphones.

### Q7. Voice consent requirements and features...
- A) Never change.
- B) Should be verified with the provider before you rely on them.
- C) Don't apply to real estate.

**Answer:** B — Check current terms and features in your account.
