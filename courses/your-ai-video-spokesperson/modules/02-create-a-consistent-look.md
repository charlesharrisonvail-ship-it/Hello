# Module 2 — Create a Consistent Look

**Course:** Your AI Video Spokesperson · **Status:** draft manuscript · **Video:** not yet recorded

## 1. Learning objective and required inputs

**By the end of this module you can** lock a master look (framing, lighting, wardrobe,
background) from approved references, and plan two controlled variations that keep your
spokesperson recognizable.

**You need:**
- Your Spokesperson Brief and Rights Checklist.
- `resources/visual-style-sheet.md`.
- Own-likeness route: a phone or webcam that records at least 1080p at 30 fps, a quiet
  room, and soft even light. Fictional route: your licensed character's reference images.

## 2. Narrated lesson script

*Narrator: Eva Vail, AiRE Estate's AI presenter, using owner-approved Eva assets and voice. Target read time about 6–7 minutes.*

Think about the agents whose videos you recognize in your feed. You probably know them
in half a second, before they say a word. Same framing, same kind of light, often the
same jacket. That's not an accident. It's consistency, and it's what makes repeated
videos feel like one person instead of a slideshow of strangers.

With AI avatars, consistency matters even more, because small changes compound. Change
the background, and the lighting the AI infers changes. Change the jacket and the hair
at the same time, and suddenly the avatar in video three looks like a cousin of the one
in video one.

So we lock a master look, and we change one thing at a time.

The master look has eight parts on the Style Sheet. Framing: waist up or head and
shoulders, centered, eyes in the upper third. Camera height: eye level. Lighting: soft
and even, from the front or slightly to one side. Wardrobe: a solid color, no big logos,
no busy patterns. Hair and grooming: as you normally look. Accessories: go easy;
HeyGen's own guidance says glasses and large accessories can create artifacts. Background:
plain and static. And your expression at rest: a slight, natural smile, eyes on the lens.

If you're on the own-likeness route, those choices are made when you film. HeyGen's
guidance, as we checked it for this course, is simple: at least 1080p at thirty frames
per second, landscape or portrait, bright even light, a simple static background, a quiet
room, eye contact with the lens, and one continuous recording with no cuts. It suggests
recording at least two minutes, and up to five for the best results. Whatever's in the
footage shows up in the avatar, so a cough, an odd movement or an echo will be there
forever. Do one or two warm-up takes, then the real one.

If you're on the fictional route, your master look is your reference image set: the same
character, same framing, same light, used every time.

Let me show you why one change at a time matters. Here's a master look: navy jacket, grey
background, waist up. Here's a controlled variation: same everything, but the background
is a softly out-of-focus bookshelf. Still clearly the same person. Now here's an
uncontrolled one: new jacket, different hairstyle, outdoors, closer framing. Each change
was reasonable on its own. Together, it reads as somebody else.

That's exactly how my own look is managed. My face, hair and framing stay locked, and only
the setting changes for different topics. Watch the setting change and the person stay
the same. Please don't copy me; copy the method.

Now plan two settings. On the Style Sheet, each planned setting lists what changes and
what stays locked. For example: Office, where only the background changes, for
introductions. Outdoor, where the background and light change, matched carefully, for
listing invitations. Two settings are plenty to start.

Two last things for your brand. Decide where your logo goes and keep it out of the
caption area. And keep your brokerage identification consistent from video to video.

Your exercise: fill in the master look, capture or select your master reference frame,
and plan two settings. Then put the master and both settings side by side on your phone.
If someone who doesn't know you would think they're the same person, you're done.

## 4. Written walkthrough

**Verified October 23, 2026** against HeyGen's help center ("Create your first Digital
Twin (Video Avatar) with Avatar IV!").

**Own likeness:**
1. Choose your wardrobe: solid color, no large logos or text.
2. Set up soft, even light facing you; avoid harsh shadows and overexposure.
3. Choose a plain, static background; quiet room, no other voices.
4. Set your phone or camera to at least 1080p, 30 fps; landscape (16:9) or portrait (9:16).
5. Frame yourself per your Style Sheet; camera at eye level.
6. Do one or two warm-up takes. Then record one continuous take of at least 2 minutes
   (up to 5 recommended), speaking naturally, looking into the lens, keeping gestures in
   frame. Don't stop or cut; if you slip, keep going or restart from the beginning.
7. Save a still frame from the take as your master reference.

**Fictional route:**
1. Select or create your character's reference images under a license that covers
   commercial use; save the license.
2. Choose one image as the master reference.

**Both routes:** fill in Style Sheet sections 1–5 and save your master and two planned
settings.

## 5. Copy-ready template — variation request

Use this wording in your notes, or in a provider's "look" prompt where one exists:

```
Keep identical to the master look: face, hair, wardrobe, framing, camera height,
expression at rest.
Change only: [ONE ELEMENT, e.g., background to a softly out-of-focus bookshelf].
Match the lighting direction and color of the master.
No text, signs, logos or other people in the background.
```

   d) Glasses added, outdoor setting, different hairstyle.

## 7. Worked example — and why it meets the criteria

- Master: waist up; eye level; soft light from camera left; solid navy jacket; no
  accessories; plain light-grey background; slight smile.
- Setting 1, Office: background → out-of-focus bookshelf; everything else locked.
- Setting 2, Outdoor porch: background and light source change, light matched to the same
  direction and softness; wardrobe, hair, framing locked.
- Proposed changes: a) controlled; b) not (two changes); c) controlled; d) not (three changes).

**Why it meets the criteria:** each setting changes one identity-safe element, and the
identity-defining features (face, hair, wardrobe, framing) stay locked.

## 8. Troubleshooting and completion checklist

**My footage has an echo.** Re-record in a smaller room with soft furnishings; the avatar
keeps the echo.

**The avatar's hands glitch.** Keep gestures inside the frame and away from your face.

**Completion checklist**
- [ ] Master look filled in and reference frame saved.
- [ ] Two planned settings, one change each.
- [ ] Logo and brokerage identification placement decided.
- [ ] Phone side-by-side check done.

## 9. Module check

### Q1. Why lock a master look?
- A) To save storage.
- B) Repeated videos feel like one recognizable person; small changes compound.
- C) Providers require it.
- D) It makes videos longer.
**Answer:** B — Consistency builds recognition.

### Q2. Which change is controlled?
- A) New jacket, new hair and new background together.
- B) Only the background changes; everything else stays the same.
- C) Different person.
- D) New framing, new outfit and outdoor light.
**Answer:** B — One element at a time.

### Q3. Per HeyGen's filming guidance (checked Oct. 23, 2026), what minimum recording quality is suggested?
- A) 480p at 15 fps.
- B) At least 1080p at 30 fps.
- C) 8K at 120 fps only.
- D) Any quality.
**Answer:** B — Higher (e.g., 4K, 60 fps) is better, but 1080p/30 is the floor.

### Q4. You stumble on a word during your recording. What does HeyGen's guidance say to do?
- A) Stop, cut out the mistake, and splice the clip.
- B) Keep speaking naturally or restart from the beginning; don't stitch clips.
- C) Add a filter.
- D) Submit two separate clips.
**Answer:** B — The footage must be one continuous recording.

### Q5. Why avoid clothing with large logos or text?
- A) Brands may sue.
- B) It can cause artifacts or distortions in the avatar, and distracts from you.
- C) It's illegal.
- D) It reduces resolution.
**Answer:** B — HeyGen's FAQ notes logos and text may cause artifacts.

### Q6. What may students take from the Eva Vail example?
- A) Eva's face.
- B) Eva's voice.
- C) The method: lock identity, vary the setting.
- D) Eva's name.
**Answer:** C — Copy the method, never the person.

### Q7. How many planned settings does this module suggest starting with?
- A) Ten.
- B) Two.
- C) None.
- D) One per video.
**Answer:** B — Two controlled settings are plenty to start.
