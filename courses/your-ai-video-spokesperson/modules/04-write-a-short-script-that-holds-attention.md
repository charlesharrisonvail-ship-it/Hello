# Module 4 — Write a Short Script That Holds Attention

**Course:** Your AI Video Spokesperson · **Status:** draft manuscript · **Video:** not yet recorded

## 1. Learning objective and required inputs

**By the end of this module you can** write a 15–30 second script with a hook, one useful
point, evidence where a claim needs it, and one call to action, and time it by reading it
aloud.

**You need:**
- `resources/short-script-templates.md` (three patterns, three fictional worked examples).
- The fictional listing sheet for 1418 Juniper Lane (from Build Your AI Assistant's
  practice data, or the facts repeated below).
- A timer (your phone).

## 2. Narrated lesson script

*Narrator: Eva Vail, AiRE Estate's AI presenter, using owner-approved Eva assets and voice. Target read time about 7 minutes.*

A short video has about three seconds to earn the next three. Most agent videos spend
those seconds on "Hey everyone, happy Tuesday, so today I wanted to talk about..." By
"talk," the viewer's gone.

Every script in this course has four parts. A hook. One useful point. Evidence, where the
point needs it. And one call to action.

The hook is the first line, and it should tell the right viewer this is for them.
"Selling in Willow Fork next year?" "Before you list, find your HOA documents." Not
"Hi, I'm Sam." Your name can come second.

One useful point. One. Not three tips, not a market overview. One thing the viewer can
use. If you have three tips, you have three videos. That's good news, not a problem.

Evidence. If your point includes a fact, the fact needs a source. "Buyers often ask for
HOA documents early" is general, careful, and fine without a statistic. "Homes with HOA
documents ready sell twenty-two percent faster" needs a real source you can name. If you
don't have one, don't say it. AI tools will happily invent a confident percentage. Don't
let them.

One call to action. "Message me." "Comment HOA and I'll send the checklist." "Open house
Sunday, one to three." Pick one. Two calls to action is zero calls to action.

Now, length. People often say "a hundred and fifty words is a minute," and that's a rough
starting point at best. Your spokesperson's pace, the pauses you added in Module 3, and
how many numbers you say all change the time. So read every script aloud at the pace
you'll use and time it. That's the real length. Aim for fifteen to thirty seconds.

There are three patterns in the templates file, each with a fully worked fictional example.

The introduction. Hook for who it's for, who you are, and a plain AI disclosure: "this is
an AI version of me, made from my own recording, so I can post short tips more often. I
write and review every one." Then one CTA.

The educational tip. One mistake or question, the one thing to do, careful wording, one
CTA. Notice the words "often" and "can." Not "always" and "will." Careful wording is how
you stay accurate without a statistic.

The listing invitation. Hook with one true, specific feature from the listing sheet.
Then the facts: address, beds, baths, square feet, one more. Then the open house or
"message me." Every fact must match the listing sheet. And if it's a real listing, you
need the seller's permission to market it, and your MLS and brokerage rules apply.

Now the list of things that never go in a script. Invented testimonials. "My clients
always say..." unless it's a real, permitted, verifiable quote. Firsthand experiences the
speaker didn't have, which matters a lot for fictional spokespeople. Guaranteed results.
Anything about who should live somewhere, school quality claims, or neighborhood "type."
And statistics without a source you can name.

You can use AI to draft scripts. Here's the catch: AI drafts tend to break exactly those
rules. They open with "Hey there!", they add a third tip, and they invent a percentage.
The prompt in this lesson tells it the structure and the rules, and you still read every
line against the listing sheet.

Your exercise: write three scripts, one per pattern, for the fictional practice listing.
Time each one aloud. Mark every fact and its source. That's your Approved Script Pack.

## 4. Written walkthrough

1. Open the templates file and choose a pattern.
2. Write the hook first. Read it alone: does the right viewer know it's for them?
3. Write the one point. If you have more, start a second script.
4. For every fact, write its source beside the script (listing sheet, official page). If
   you can't name a source, use careful general wording or cut it.
5. Write one CTA.
6. Read aloud at your spokesperson's pace and time it with your phone. Trim to 15–30 s.
7. Check against the "never" list.
8. Save the script, its time, and its sources in your Script Pack.

**Practice listing facts (fictional, 1418 Juniper Lane, Willow Fork):** list price
$612,000; 4 bed / 2.5 bath; 2,240 finished sq ft; 0.21-acre lot; built 1998; 2-car
attached garage; open house Sunday 1–3 p.m. HOA fee not confirmed (don't state it).

## 5. Copy-ready prompt

```
Draft a short video script for a real estate agent. Pattern: [INTRODUCTION / TIP /
LISTING INVITATION]. Structure: a hook in the first sentence; one useful point; evidence
only if a fact is stated; one call to action: [CTA].
Rules: use only these facts: [PASTE FACTS WITH SOURCES]. Don't add any number, feature,
statistic, testimonial or personal experience. No guarantees. No statements about
schools, neighborhood character, or who a home is "perfect for." Include this AI
disclosure line if the pattern is INTRODUCTION: [LINE].
Target: about 15–30 seconds read aloud. Return the script, then a list of each fact used
and its source.
```

## 7. Worked example — and why it meets the criteria

See the three worked examples in `short-script-templates.md`. The listing invitation:

> A backyard you'll actually use, in Willow Fork.
> 1418 Juniper Lane: four bedrooms, two and a half baths, about 2,240 finished square
> feet, and a two-car garage.
> Open house Sunday, one to three.
> Message me for details. Sam Reyes, Willow Fork Realty.

*Facts and sources:* address, beds, baths, sq ft, garage — listing sheet; open house —
showing schedule. *Timed:* ~20 s.

**Why it meets the criteria:** hook in the first line; one point; every fact sourced; one
CTA; brokerage identification; no HOA fee (not confirmed), no school or neighborhood
claims. "A backyard you'll actually use" is a soft hook; if your broker considers it a
claim, cut it to "Backyard, garage and four bedrooms in Willow Fork."

## 8. Troubleshooting and completion checklist

**My script runs 45 seconds.** Cut the first sentence. Then cut the second point.

**The AI added a statistic.** Delete it unless you can name and verify the source.

**Completion checklist**
- [ ] Three scripts, one per pattern.
- [ ] Each timed aloud at 15–30 s.
- [ ] Every fact has a source.
- [ ] One CTA each; disclosure in the introduction.
- [ ] Nothing from the "never" list.

## 9. Module check

### Q1. Which is the strongest hook for sellers?
- A) "Hey everyone, happy Tuesday!"
- B) "Selling in Willow Fork next year? Start here."
- C) "So, um, today I wanted to talk about something."
- D) "I'm Sam."
**Answer:** B — It tells the right viewer it's for them, in the first seconds.

### Q2. You have three tips. What should you do?
- A) Squeeze all three into 20 seconds.
- B) Make three videos, one point each.
- C) Pick the longest one.
- D) Use a faster voice.
**Answer:** B — One useful point per video.

### Q3. An AI draft says "Homes with HOA documents ready sell 22% faster." You have no source. What do you do?
- A) Keep it; it sounds right.
- B) Delete it, or replace it with careful general wording.
- C) Round it to 20%.
- D) Add "studies show."
**Answer:** B — Unsourced statistics don't go in your videos.

### Q4. How should you determine a script's length?
- A) 150 words always equals one minute.
- B) Read it aloud at the spokesperson's pace and time it.
- C) Count characters.
- D) Let the provider decide.
**Answer:** B — Word counts are only a starting guess.

### Q5. Which line belongs in a fictional spokesperson's script?
- A) "When I bought my first home..."
- B) "My clients always tell me..." (no real quote)
- C) "Before you list, find your HOA documents."
- D) "We guarantee a sale in 30 days."
**Answer:** C — Useful and true; no invented experiences, testimonials or guarantees.

### Q6. A listing invitation states the HOA fee as "$40/month." The fee isn't confirmed. What's the problem?
- A) None.
- B) It's an unconfirmed fact; leave it out until it's documented.
- C) It should be $45.
- D) HOA fees are never mentioned.
**Answer:** B — Facts come from approved sources only.

### Q7. How many calls to action should a 20-second video have?
- A) One.
- B) Two.
- C) Three.
- D) None.
**Answer:** A — Two CTAs compete; one gets done.
