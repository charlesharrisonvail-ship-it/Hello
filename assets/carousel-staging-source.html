Staging at
Altitude

5 moves that sell mountain homes faster —
and why suburban staging advice fails up here.

SWIPE →
EPIVAILCharles Harrison, Epique Area/Growth Leader
1

Frame the view.
Don't compete with it.

Bare windows. Clear sightlines from the front door to the glass. Furniture angled toward the mountains, not the TV.

The view is the most expensive thing in the house — staging that blocks it is staging that costs money.

EPIVAIL2 / 7
2

Stage the fireplace like it's the kitchen.

In resort markets, it is. Buyers picture snowy evenings before they picture dinner parties.

Wood stacked, hearth styled, seating pulled in close — make it the room's gravity.

EPIVAIL3 / 7
3

Texture over color.

Wool throws. Timber. Stone. Leather with some years on it.

Mountain buyers touch everything — a home that feels like a lodge outsells one that photographs like a catalog.

EPIVAIL4 / 7
4

Show the après moments.

The mudroom with boot warmers. The hot-tub deck. The gear wall.

Suburban staging hides utility spaces. Up here, utility spaces are the lifestyle — and the lifestyle is what's for sale.

EPIVAIL5 / 7
5

Light for 4pm winter.
Not noon summer.

Warm lamps on. Fire going. Golden hour, staged indoors.

That's the hour buyers dream about owning here — show the home at the moment it sells itself.
