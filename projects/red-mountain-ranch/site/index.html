01 The place

A ranch that
keeps its river.

Parcel 1 is 17.5 acres of old ranch land on the north bank of the Eagle River, east-northeast of U.S. 6 and Nogal Road. It slopes gently from the highway down to the water, with old-growth tree clusters along the way.

The plan keeps the land open: homes sit at the edges, a 75-foot setback protects the riverbank, and the public Discovery Trail runs through it, linking all six Red Mountain Ranch parcels and giving residents a direct way to the water. The Eagle Valley Trail connects to downtown Eagle.

02 The homes

Sixty-six homes,
and room to breathe.

The recorded plan allowed up to 97 homes on this parcel. The design team chose 66 to keep the open space, the river corridor and the trees. Select a home type.

Forty-two townhomes.

Eight townhome buildings in four designs, reached from the main two-way road off U.S. 6 along the upper edge of the site.

  • BedroomsThree or four, per the developer
  • ParkingTwo-car garage with every home
  • PricingNot yet set (“too early to say”)

MDP narrative, Jan 13, 2025; Vail Daily, Apr 2, 2026.

Twelve duplex homes.

Six duplex buildings set on a quieter one-way inner road, closer to the open space at the heart of the site.

  • BedroomsThree or four, per the developer
  • ParkingTwo-car garage with every home
  • SettingInner road, facing the preserved core

MDP narrative, Jan 13, 2025; PPR narrative, Sep 2025.

Twelve single-family homes.

Twelve detached homes in two designs, also on the inner road. About 218,000 square feet of new homes across the parcel, with no fences or private yards; the land between is shared, native open space.

  • BedroomsThree or four, per the developer
  • LandscapeNative planting, no irrigation once established
  • Roofs30% dedicated to solar

MDP narrative, Jan 13, 2025; PPR narrative, Sep 2025; Vail Daily, Apr 2, 2026.

03 The EpiVail approach

A first parcel deserves
a first-class launch.

The developer has called Parcel 1 a proof of concept for six river parcels. What follows is a proposal for how EpiVail could help prove it, offered with respect for the work already done. Nothing here is engaged, appointed or promised.

  1. i.

    A story people can feel.

    River, trails, open land, all-electric homes: a place story built on what the plan really delivers, using approved names, artwork and facts only.

  2. ii.

    A digital home before the first footing.

    A site like this one, updated as permits move, so early buyers can follow the project and see exactly what is planned.

  3. iii.

    Interest, gathered and kept warm.

    When authorized, a buyer interest list with careful CRM follow-up, ready for the day pricing and reservations open.

  4. iv.

    A launch the valley notices.

    Listings, on-site previews, short-form video and the broker community, timed to the real construction schedule.

  5. v.

    One partner for all six parcels.

    A consistent presence from Parcel 1 forward, so each new phase starts with buyers who already know the name.

Town of Eagle · Submitted by the architect, Tres Birds · Item 02, project narrative

Sixty-six homes, by choice.

What it establishes

66 homes (42 townhomes, 12 duplex, 12 single-family) in 26 buildings, about 218,420 sq ft, where the recorded PUD allows 97. Commits Parcel OS-1 to the town as a public park. Utilities from the Town of Eagle and Holy Cross Energy.

What it does not

Pricing, sale terms, construction dates, or final approval. It is the applicant's submittal.

Town of Eagle · Item 06, general development information · Dated Sep 17–22, 2025

Designed to fit the river corridor.

What it establishes

The 75-foot river setback and Discovery Trail; density cut by 31 homes to honor the 2015 Eagle River Corridor Plan; all-electric homes with heat pumps, envelopes 10% better than code, 30% of roof space for solar, and native, low-water landscaping.
