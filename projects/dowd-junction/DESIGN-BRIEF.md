# Creative direction: riverfront mountain editorial

## What failed
The rejected version was a generic brochure: flat SVG mountain art, a schematic that felt like a placeholder, repetitive headings, undifferentiated blocks and typography-only branding. It did not convey the physical site or demonstrate premium real estate marketing. Do not retain the invented “Development Collection” brand extension as though it were established.

## Desired impression
An experienced local real estate professional has thought deeply about this place and can present it beautifully. Sophisticated and grounded, not flashy proptech and not a generic luxury-condo sales page. This is community housing, not a proven luxury offering.

## Visual system
- Full-bleed photographic opening: authentic Eagle River / EagleVail / Dowd Junction imagery preferred. Use a strong crop with space for type; subtle gradient only to protect readability.
- Editorial serif for large headlines with a clean sans for navigation/body. Suggested licensed typefaces: Cormorant Garamond + Manrope, or suitable high-quality alternatives. Bundle or use reliable fallbacks.
- Warm ivory, deep charcoal/pine, muted bronze; avoid the original flat green-on-green design and generic neon accents.
- Asymmetry, thoughtful image/text overlaps, fine rules, substantial margins, strong typography and varied section rhythm. No sea of rounded cards or icon grids.
- Restrained motion on scrolling and transitions. No scroll hijacking, auto-playing sound, distracting particles or looping stock video.
- Official EpiVail/Epique logos if supplied; otherwise clean temporary text branding. Never fabricate an official mark.

## Suggested content progression
1. Place-first hero: “A new chapter on the Eagle River.” Eyebrow: “Dowd Junction · A development marketing concept by EpiVail.” CTA: “Explore the vision.” These are creative suggestions, not an official project name.
2. Location story: river setting, Highway 6 corridor, relationship to EagleVail, Avon and Vail. Use real map/source imagery with attribution, not an invented parcel drawing. No unverified drive times, ski access, private river access or walkability claims.
3. Opportunity: approximately 100 homes envisioned initially and 200 in a future northern phase, as dated planning goals. Make phase selection informative, not merely a decorative counter.
4. EpiVail pitch: place branding; a dedicated digital experience; interest-building and CRM follow-up when authorized; clear approved availability and eligibility; local outreach. Present these as proposed services.
5. Public plans: clean document panel with title, date, page pointer, what it establishes, and working source link. Offer original PDFs only if actually retrieved and verified.
6. Conversation CTA: Charles Harrison, EpiVail / Epique Realty; direct link to his real estate website. No made-up email or phone number.
7. Compact source/status note. Optional short FAQ; do not let caveats dominate the marketing experience.

## Functional requirements
Responsive nav, meaningful section anchors, accessible phase tabs, verified outbound document links, contact CTA, useful mobile design. No personal-data collection in this static pitch. An optional form demo must clearly explain that it sends nothing and should contain no real prospect information.

## Acceptance criteria
- Looks distinctly better than the original at first glance: excellent photographic art direction and typography, not a template reskin.
- No SVG mountain placeholder hero, fake architectural renderings or irrelevant skyline photos.
- Every major section adds new substance and has a distinct composition.
- Proposal status is clear without a wall of warnings.
- No invented market facts, official appointment, testimonials or operating backend.
- Test 390/768/1440 widths, navigation, tab keyboard behavior, contact/document links, reduced motion, contrast and overflow. State actual test coverage honestly.
- Ship source + README with exact install/run/build commands + asset credits + unresolved-items list.
