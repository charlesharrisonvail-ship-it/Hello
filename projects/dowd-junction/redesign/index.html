01 The place

Where the valley
gathers itself.

Dowd Junction is the narrow bend where Interstate 70 meets U.S. 24, the Eagle River turns, and the road to Minturn begins. Just downstream, U.S. Highway 6 follows the river west through EagleVail toward Avon.

Public planning documents describe the former CDOT employee-housing site here, between Highway 6 and the river, as a place for community housing. It sits in the working heart of the central Vail Valley, with a mountain backdrop on every side.

03 The EpiVail approach

A place deserves a story,
and a plan to tell it.

What follows is a proposal, offered with respect for the work already underway. Nothing here is engaged, appointed or promised. It is simply how I would set about it.

  1. i.

    A name and a sense of place.

    Place branding grounded in the river, the corridor and the people the homes will serve. Built only on approved names, artwork and facts.

  2. ii.

    A dedicated digital home.

    A clear, beautiful website that answers the real questions: where, how many, for whom, and when. Updated as approvals move.

  3. iii.

    Interest, gathered with care.

    When authorized, an interest list and thoughtful CRM follow-up, so local workers hear first and nobody falls through the cracks.

  4. iv.

    Availability and eligibility, made plain.

    Approved pricing, tenure and eligibility rules explained in plain language, so the right households apply with confidence.

  5. v.

    Local outreach, in person.

    Introductions to valley employers, community groups and neighbors, carried by someone who lives and works in the Colorado Mountain Region.

04 Grounded in the public record

The plans behind
the possibility.

Town of Avon · Planning & Zoning Commission · Printed page 27, “State Land Board”

The 300-home goal, in two phases.

What it establishes

A goal of about 300 community-housing homes: an early phase of 100 between U.S. 6 and the Eagle River, and a future phase of 200 north of the river. It names annexation, zoning and subdivision as needed steps and includes a parcel illustration.

What it does not

Approved inventory, tenure, pricing, or a developer appointment. Acreage labels differ from later transaction reporting.

Town of Avon · Printed pages 9–10 (PDF pages 11–12)

Where the idea first took shape.

What it establishes

The eastern CDOT housing area had been vacated, with about 96 deed-restricted homes envisioned. The western portion remained a CDOT service yard at the time.

What it does not

The current unit goal. It is earlier planning context, since refined by the 2026 packet.

Town of Avon · Revised annexation petition, legal descriptions, phase-one boundary maps

The formal path toward annexation.

What it establishes

A prior petition was withdrawn and a smaller-area petition submitted. The resolution initiates annexation proceedings and includes boundary exhibits.

What it does not

Completed annexation, construction approval or current ownership.

Town of Vail · Vail Home Partners · Final page, January 1 update

Why there is a Corum sign on site.

What it establishes

Corum was using off-site storage at Dowd Junction to help address soil conditions at Southface Vail, its separate project near Middle Creek in Vail.

What it does not

That Corum is the developer of the Dowd Junction housing, or the exact storage parcel. That role remains to be confirmed.

Questions I would bring to the first conversation

  • Where do annexation and the land purchase stand today?
  • Who will develop the homes, and is there a selection process underway?
  • For sale, for rent, or both, and for which households?
  • How will sales or leasing representation be chosen?
  • What names, plans and artwork are approved to share?
